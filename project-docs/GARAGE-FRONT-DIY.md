# Garage front — efficient DIY plan

Planning issue: 12 September 2026. This is a scoped work plan and budget, not an engineered construction drawing or a final shopping/cut list.

## Selected approach

Viewed from the driveway: retain the left pair of outward-swinging timber doors, retain the middle structural post and the existing header, and enclose the right bay. Put one stock insulated 36 × 80 in. prehung steel entrance door near the far-right side. Show inward swing toward the right wall in the model; confirm handing, clearance, finished floor height and the manufacturer's frame dimensions before ordering. Leave room for the new jamb framing and exterior trim beside the existing corner.

Use a simple painted solid door and engineered-wood panel siding for the new wall. Match the existing white trim and blue-gray siding. Spend first on weather protection, sound timber and dependable hardware. Do not add decorative glazing, custom metalwork, whole-garage insulation or roof alterations to this phase.

Repair the left leaves only if the stiles, rails, joints and timber at the hinge fixings are sound. The photos show lower-edge deterioration but do not establish remaining strength. Reusing sound components is the low-cost route; rebuilding large glazed leaves is not automatically cheaper after hardware, glazing and joinery are included.

[Westfield zoning requirements](https://www.westfieldnj.gov/2167/Zoning-Permits), [Building & Construction](https://www.westfieldnj.gov/547/Building-Construction).
2. Establish the paint hazard before scraping, sanding or cutting the old painted doors/trim. If the building age or paint history is unknown, treat the coating as potentially lead-containing until assessed. Follow a lead-safe work plan; do not dry-sand or power-wash deteriorating paint. EPA recommends assessment by a certified inspector/risk assessor. [EPA DIY lead precautions](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-safe-renovations-diyers).
3. Leave the existing siding and unknown sheet materials undisturbed until identified. Appearance alone cannot rule out asbestos. If the flashing tie-in will disturb suspect material, use an accredited asbestos professional to assess it; do not sample, saw, drill or sand it yourself. [EPA asbestos guidance](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos/protect-your-family-exposures-asbestos).
4. Obtain a field check of post/header/jamb condition and the proposed wall-to-slab attachment. A cracked or shallow slab edge, soft post base, visibly moving joint or active water entry stops that part of the work. No post cutting, header replacement, roof-member removal or unverified concrete anchoring is included here.

## Build sequence — right bay

Proceed through these work packages only after the gates above are cleared. Allow curing/drying time stated by the products, and do not leave the opening unsecured overnight.

### 1. Measure and mark

- Mark the new wall plane, exact door rough opening and inward swing on the floor with tape.
- Check that the door clears the right wall, equipment and planned floor finish through its full swing.
- Inspect the outside approach during/after rain. Resolve water entering at the threshold before concealing the base.
- Record the selected door SKU, handing, jamb depth, unit size and rough-opening size on the job sheet. Confirm sill-pan and finished-threshold heights still fit below the existing header.

**Done when:** the field measurements and selected door drawing agree; the anchoring and water-management details are approved.

### 2. Remove only the right door leaves

- Arrange a helper and rated support/lifting equipment for the heavy glazed leaves. Support each leaf before releasing hardware; never depend on the remaining hinge alone.
- Label reusable hardware, protect the glass and store each removed leaf safely on suitable supports away from the work route.
- Retain the existing center post, header and structural jambs. Remove only trim specifically identified for the approved tie-in after the hazard assessment.

**Done when:** the bay is accessible, existing structural timber is undamaged and the opening can be secured/weather-covered.

### 3. Frame and anchor the infill

- Use straight kiln-dried framing lumber; use suitable preservative-treated timber where the bottom plate contacts the concrete, separated by a compatible capillary-break/sill gasket.
- Form the door opening to its manufacturer's actual rough-opening dimensions. Frame the narrow right jamb zone and the solid panel to the left; retain the existing header above.
- Plumb and square the frame before permanent attachment. Do not wedge the new wall up to lift or preload the old header.
- Attach to sound existing framing and verified concrete with the approved connector/anchor system. Select corrosion-compatible fasteners for treated wood. Anchor diameter, embedment, spacing and edge distances are intentionally **not specified from photographs**. Do not anchor into asphalt, damaged slab edges or unverified concrete. Do not substitute drywall screws for rated framing connections.

**Done when:** required framing/attachment inspection is accepted before it is covered.

### 4. Make a continuous drainage layer

- Install rated exterior structural sheathing with supported seams and the approved fastening pattern. Its thickness and fastening schedule are not a substitute for the approved bracing detail.
- Integrate new water-resistive barrier (WRB) with the existing drainage plane at both sides and above. Upper layers overlap lower layers so water exits outward; surface caulk alone is not the tie-in.
- Install compatible base flashing and the door sill pan, with back/end dams and an outward drainage path appropriate to the actual slab/landing condition.
- Prepare jamb/head interfaces as the selected system requires. Coordinate final flashing and drip-cap installation with setting the door in the next step; the before/after-unit sequence varies by product. Follow one compatible WRB/flashing system and the selected door instructions. [DuPont installation resources](https://www.dupont.com/building/how-to-install.html).

**Done when:** the flashing connections are inspectable, continuous and drain outward before siding hides them.

### 5. Install the stock entrance door

- With a helper, dry-fit the prehung unit and check pan/threshold support.
- Apply the selected system's sealant; set the unit, plumb the hinge jamb and shim at prescribed support/fixing locations.
- Fasten using the door manufacturer's instructions. Check even gaps, weatherstrip contact, smooth swing and latch engagement before the final fastening.
- Complete the jamb/head flashing and drip-cap integration at the specified stage for the unit and WRB system. Upper layers lap over lower ones; leave the manufacturer's outward drainage exits open.
- Air-seal the interior frame gap with compatible low-expansion window-and-door foam or the specified alternative. Finish the threshold and exterior perimeter as instructed without blocking drainage.
- Paint all surfaces required by the manufacturer within its stated time window. Install the lockset and adjust the sweep/threshold.

**Done when:** the door opens without rubbing, latches without lifting/pushing, and seals evenly. Exact clearances and fixing details are SKU-specific. [Manufacturer installation resources](https://www.thermatru.com/project-center/product-installation/), [JELD-WEN prehung installation guide — reference, verify current SKU instructions](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c1/c13373e8-6f59-4217-a7da-0ef923710fda.pdf).

### 6. Clad, finish and inspect

- Fit engineered-wood panel siding and simple trim, leaving the selected product's gaps/clearances. Prime and paint exposed cuts before assembly where practical.
- Keep wood-based siding above the driveway/ground clearance required by its instructions. Use a correctly flashed durable base detail; PVC trim does not repair an underlying drainage problem.
- Paint the new wall, trim and restored left leaves as a coordinated front elevation.
- After sealants have cured, check the inside after rainfall for water entry. Correct defects before insulation or internal lining. Complete the required inspections.

**Done when:** no rubbing, unsecured leaves, exposed cuts, trapped drainage or observed water entry remains. LP's instructions require clearance from grade/hard surfaces and product-specific fastening; confirm the exact panel issue at purchase. [LP manufacturer application instructions](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/lp/LP-SmartSide-Trim-Siding-Installation-Instructions-2368112.pdf).

## Left doors — repair decision and work order

**Repair route:** retain sound frame joints and existing glazing; replace only isolated nonstructural boarding/trim; renew failed weatherseals and appropriately rated hardware; refinish after lead-safe preparation. Reuse hinges only if condition, capacity and attachment are adequate. Weatherseal both outer jambs/head, the meeting edge with an astragal system, and the bottom with a compatible sweep/threshold arrangement that clears the asphalt.

**Stop the simple repair route** if a bottom rail or stile is soft, a joint opens under movement, the door twists/sags materially, or hinge fixings are in unsound wood. Filler, an exterior cover board or extra screws do not restore the door's load-bearing frame. Obtain a joinery/hardware plan before repairing those members or building a replacement leaf.

For any new large DIY leaves, release a separate drawing only after checking opening/leaf dimensions, finished weight, glazing safety, frame joinery, anti-sag bracing and wind/hold-open hardware. The brace layout and joints must be part of that drawing. Decorative X trim is not by itself a verified brace. Do not copy nominal shed-door hardware capacities to these large glazed leaves. No full door cut list is released yet.

Work on one leaf at a time with proper support. Adjust hardware and safe swing clearance before fitting weatherseals. Secure each leaf when open so wind cannot slam it. Do not add heavy insulation skins to an unverified door frame.
