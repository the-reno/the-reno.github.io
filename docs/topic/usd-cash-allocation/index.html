Where should short-term cash sit?

Historical rates

A long period of lower rates gave way to high inflation and one of the fastest Fed hiking cycles in 2022.

The curve moved from yield pickup to path risk.

The allocation dilemma

From a cash perspective, every decision starts with the same choice.

Stay short and keep the overnight rate or extend into term and lock a return for the period ahead.

The objectiveMake cash work harder over the period.
The challengePrice the future before it happens.

The elements

Today’s rateThe overnight rate represents the rate at which cash resets daily and moves closely with the Fed’s policy rate.
SpreadThe gap between overnight and term reflects expected rate moves, risk premium and liquidity for the period.
Term rateTerm rates look ahead. OIS, SOFR futures and Term SOFR are built differently, but they broadly reflect the market’s expected path for overnight rates over the period ahead.

Overnight tells us what cash earns today. Term rates put a price on that uncertainty.

What if the cycle flips?

Replay the same cycle upside down and term can switch from drag to protection.

When the path turns lower, locking term can switch from drag to protection.

Policy repricing mattersUnexpected hikes or delayed cuts can erode the benefit of locking in.
Timing drives outcomeResults improve when the market underprices future easing.

How should cash be allocated when the path is uncertain?

No single tenor wins in every scenario

Sticky paths reward term. Fast easing rewards liquidity. A diversified ladder lowers the cost of being wrong.

The decision

Set the objective — understand the trade-off.

Liquidity, yield and resilience compete for the same allocation. More of one usually means accepting less of another.

Yield · Lock today’s returnMore term increases earnings certainty, but reduces flexibility if rates or liquidity needs change.
Liquidity · Preserve access to cashMore short-term exposure increases flexibility, but leaves more return exposed to future rates.
Resilience · Reduce dependence on one rate pathA broader allocation can lower the cost of being wrong, but may give up some upside if one tenor proves best.

Build the model — capture what matters.

The model has the same trade-off: too simple can miss the risk; too complex can add assumptions, variables and noise. Add complexity where it materially improves the answer and supports the decision with confidence.
